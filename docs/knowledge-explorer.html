Reusable device knowledge

Browse what RLB already knows.

Knowledge Explorer is the browsing layer for RLB's accumulated device, firmware, hardware, DTB, panel, recipe, media and issue knowledge. It exists so each investigation can make the next one better.

KNOWLEDGE EXPLORER — ACTUAL RLB INTERFACE
Knowledge is reusable: Device identification, firmware relationships, DTBs, hardware profiles and compatibility evidence are more valuable when RLB can carry them forward into future analysis instead of rediscovering them device by device.

1. What Knowledge Explorer is for

Knowledge Explorer is the human-browsable view of the knowledge RLB has accumulated about retro handhelds, consoles and single-board-computer gaming systems.

It lets you explore known devices and related technical information independently of a live Device Inspector session.

3. Search across device and hardware knowledge

The main search field accepts device, firmware, hardware-profile, DTB, panel and recipe terms. This gives users one place to find the knowledge they need even when they do not yet know which domain contains it.

5. Device cards are grouped into recognizable families

The main device area groups entries by manufacturer or family, making large catalogs easier to browse than a single alphabetical wall of devices.

Each card provides the device name, category, manufacturer/family context, firmware hints and a knowledge-completeness indicator.

IMAGERecognition artworkA visual reference helps distinguish devices that may have similar names.
IDENTITYDevice and familyThe canonical device identity RLB uses in its knowledge model.
FIRMWAREFirmware hintsKnown supported or related firmware information.
KNOWLEDGECoverage indicatorA quick indication of how complete the available knowledge is for the entry.

6. Select an entry to inspect its Details pane

The right-hand Details pane shows the selected knowledge entry rather than forcing users to open a separate page for every device.

For a device, the details can include overview, manufacturer/family identification, hardware facts, firmware relationships and other connected evidence.

7. Connected Knowledge is the important part

A device entry is useful because it can connect to the other knowledge RLB has about that device.

8. Knowledge supports evidence-based identification

Knowledge Explorer is not meant to turn a product name into an automatic match. Its real value is giving Device Inspector more known relationships to compare against actual evidence from the source device.

For example, DTB fingerprints, board relationships, firmware compatibility and panel evidence can help differentiate devices that share similar retail names.

Unknown is valid: if the repository does not contain enough evidence to distinguish two devices, RLB should preserve that uncertainty rather than inventing a confident answer.

9. Knowledge coverage can be incomplete

Device cards can show a knowledge-percentage indicator. This is useful because not every device entry has complete firmware, hardware, media or compatibility information.

The goal is to make incompleteness visible and improvable, not to imply that every catalog entry is equally well understood.

10. Repository maintenance tools are separate from browsing

The current Beta also exposes several repository-maintenance actions from Knowledge Explorer, including rebuild, audit and artwork-normalization operations.

Those controls support development and repository hygiene, but they are not the primary Knowledge Explorer workflow. For normal use, the important experience is browsing domains, selecting entries and understanding connected knowledge.

Rebuild repositoryRegenerates the managed knowledge snapshot/repository from its configured source material.
Audit knowledgeChecks repository consistency and completeness.
Audit / Normalize artworkMaintenance operations for device-recognition media.
UI note: these maintenance controls are currently more visually prominent than they need to be. They are documented here as maintenance tools rather than core browsing actions.

11. Knowledge Explorer and Knowledge Sources are different

Knowledge Explorer shows what RLB knows. Knowledge Sources explains where that knowledge came from and how source material feeds the managed repository.

EXPLORERBrowse the resultDevices, firmware, boards, DTBs, panels, recipes, media and issues already represented in the repository.
↔
SOURCESUnderstand provenanceThe source repositories, sites and curated inputs that contribute knowledge to RLB.

Knowledge principles

✓Make each investigation reusable
✓Separate domains so devices, DTBs, firmware and hardware remain understandable
✓Connect related knowledge instead of storing isolated facts
✓Show incomplete knowledge honestly
✓Keep repository maintenance separate from normal browsing
