About the project

Preserve Once. Build Many.

Retro Library Builder is built around a simple idea: understand the device you actually own, protect what makes it recoverable, and reuse verified knowledge and content instead of starting from scratch every time.

01Understand firstDevice identity should come from evidence, not assumptions based on labels, folder names or generic operating-system files.
02Protect what mattersPreservation should account for the content and device evidence needed for recovery, not just produce another unverified copy.
03Reuse verified workContent and device knowledge already established should be reusable across later preservations and builds.
04Keep uncertainty visibleUnknown, ambiguous and conflicting evidence should stay visible instead of being converted into false confidence.

Why RLB exists

Retro handhelds and SBC gaming systems are often treated as if they were simply “an SD card full of ROMs.” In practice, original media can contain device-specific boot files, firmware configuration, DTBs, BIOS, artwork, metadata, saves, frontend configuration and other content that is difficult to reconstruct after the fact.

Multiple products may also share a name while using different boards, displays, boot configurations or firmware requirements. RLB exists so preservation and rebuilding decisions can be made from evidence rather than guesswork.

WITHOUT RLB“It looks like the same device.”Generic labels, copied files and uncertain firmware relationships.
→
WITH RLB“What evidence do we actually have?”Device identity, preservation state, verified content representation and accumulated knowledge.

What RLB is

RLB is a Windows desktop application focused on device intelligence, preservation, managed Library content, reusable knowledge and evidence-driven deployment.

◎Device InspectorExamines source media, device evidence, preservation state and readiness.
◆PreservationEstablishes verified protection for reusable and device-specific content.
▦Managed LibraryMaintains games, BIOS, media, saves and related content independently of a single device.
◉KnowledgeCollects device, firmware, DTB, hardware and compatibility intelligence with source attribution.
↻Background TasksKeeps long-running work visible while preserving durable execution state.
↗DeploymentUses preserved identity and verified content to build validated firmware targets with native routing, verification and frontend finalization.

The preservation philosophy

Preserve Once. Build Many. describes the relationship between preservation and deployment: protect the original carefully, establish reusable Library content and device knowledge, then use that verified foundation when replacement media is needed.

1PreserveProtect the original device and capture the evidence needed to understand it.
2VerifyProve important content relationships instead of relying on filename or size.
3LearnCarry forward device, firmware and hardware knowledge.
4BuildUse that verified foundation when preparing replacement media.

Current Beta focus

RLB is under active development, but Deployment is no longer merely a future concept: four Raspberry Pi 3B+ firmware tracks have completed physical acceptance, while source/import coverage and portability continue to expand independently.

1CorrectnessProtection accounting, device evidence and content routing must be right before convenience matters.
2ReliabilityLong-running work must leave durable, understandable state even when interrupted.
3CoveragePhysical media, network sources, BIOS, saves, configurations and firmware-specific layouts must be handled explicitly.
4User experienceAnalyze, Review/Preview, Apply, Verify, Resume and Deployment states must remain clear and honest.
5Production readinessAccepted tracks stay frozen against regression while new devices, source layouts and portability paths are added cautiously.
Beta means claims remain scoped. Physical Deployment acceptance, source/import coverage and cross-firmware portability are documented separately. A pass in one area is not used as proof that every adjacent workflow is complete.

Why device knowledge matters

Many retro handhelds are clones, revisions or loosely named families rather than a single fixed hardware design. Product name alone may not identify the board, panel, DTB or firmware required. Knowledge Explorer makes each investigation reusable by retaining device relationships, firmware evidence, boot files, fingerprints, compatibility information and source attribution.
