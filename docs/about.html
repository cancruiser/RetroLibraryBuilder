About the project

Preserve Once. Build Many.

Retro Library Builder is being built around a simple idea: understand the device you actually own, protect what makes it recoverable, and reuse verified knowledge and content instead of starting from scratch every time.

01 Understand first Device identity should come from evidence, not assumptions based on labels, folder names or generic operating-system files.
02 Protect what matters Preservation should account for the content and device evidence needed for recovery, not just produce another unverified copy.
03 Reuse verified work Content and device knowledge that has already been established should be reusable across later preservations and builds.
04 Keep uncertainty visible Unknown, ambiguous and conflicting evidence should stay visible instead of being converted into false confidence.

Why RLB exists

Retro handhelds and SBC gaming systems are often treated as if they were simply “an SD card full of ROMs.” In practice, the original media can contain device-specific boot files, firmware configuration, DTBs, BIOS files, artwork, metadata, saves, frontend configuration and other content that is difficult to reconstruct after the fact.

The situation becomes even more complicated when multiple devices are sold under similar names while using different boards, displays, boot configurations or firmware requirements.

RLB exists to bring those pieces together so that preservation and rebuilding decisions can be made from evidence rather than guesswork.

WITHOUT RLB“It looks like the same device.”Generic labels, copied files and uncertain firmware relationships.
→
WITH RLB“What evidence do we actually have?”Device identity, preservation state, verified content representation and accumulated knowledge.

What RLB is

RLB is a Windows desktop application focused on device intelligence, preservation, reusable Library content and deployment readiness.

◎Device InspectorExamines source media, device evidence, preservation state and readiness.
◆PreservationEstablishes verified protection for content and device-specific evidence.
▦LibraryMaintains reusable verified content independently of a single preserved device.
◉Knowledge ExplorerCollects device, firmware, DTB, hardware and compatibility knowledge for reuse.
↻Background TasksKeeps long-running work visible while preserving durable execution state.
↗DeploymentUses preserved identity and verified content to support safer replacement-media workflows as Beta hardening progresses.

The preservation philosophy

The phrase Preserve Once. Build Many. reflects the intended relationship between preservation and later deployment.

The original device should be inspected and protected carefully. Once RLB has established reusable device knowledge and verified Library content, that work should reduce the amount of rediscovery and duplication required for future builds.

1PreserveProtect the original device and capture the evidence needed to understand it.
2VerifyProve the content relationships that matter instead of relying on weak filename or size matches.
3LearnCarry forward device, firmware and hardware knowledge.
4BuildUse that verified foundation when preparing replacement media.

Current Beta focus

RLB is under active development. The immediate Beta work is deliberately focused on making core protection trustworthy before expanding the public deployment workflow.

1CorrectnessProtection accounting and device evidence must be right before speed or convenience matters.
2ReliabilityLong-running work must leave durable, understandable state even when interrupted.
3PerformanceLarge preservations need to complete in practical time without exhausting memory or locking the UI.
4User experienceAnalyze, Preview, Apply, Preservation, Resume and Deployment states need to be clear and honest.
5Production readinessOnly after the core protection model is stable should broader public deployment guidance become authoritative.
Beta means the application is still changing. Website guides follow validated behaviour. Planned workflows are marked as upcoming rather than documented as if they were finished.

Why device knowledge matters

Many retro handhelds are clones, revisions or loosely named families rather than a single fixed hardware design. A product name alone may not tell you which board, display panel, DTB or firmware configuration is actually required.

RLB’s Knowledge Explorer is intended to make each investigation reusable. Known device relationships, firmware evidence, boot files, DTB fingerprints and compatibility information can become part of the accumulated knowledge used in later analysis.
