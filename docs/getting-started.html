Getting Started

RLB begins by understanding the device and its media. This guide explains the workflow and the important decisions before preservation begins.

Beta software: Use RLB conservatively with original media. Do not treat a saved Device Report or a partial operation as proof that a preservation completed successfully.

Before you begin

RLB is designed around protecting original content rather than modifying it unnecessarily. Before working with important original media, make sure the destination you intend to use for preservation has sufficient free space and remains connected for the operation.

Where practical, keep the original device media available until RLB has completed and verified the protection you intended to create.

1. Start with Device Inspector

Device Inspector is the entry point for understanding what RLB is looking at. It gathers evidence from the mounted or connected media and builds a device report.

Depending on what is available, that evidence can include storage and partition layout, filesystem structure, boot assets, DTB fingerprints, firmware characteristics, ROM/content organization and known device signatures.

Why evidence matters: Product names in the retro-handheld market are not always reliable indicators of internal hardware. RLB is designed to avoid identifying a device from a folder name, volume label or generic operating-system word alone.

2. Review the identification

When RLB identifies a device, review the result rather than treating the device name as the only important part. The supporting evidence tells you why RLB reached that conclusion and helps expose uncertainty when the evidence is incomplete.

3. Understand the Device Report

A Device Report records what RLB learned during inspection. It is useful historical information, but it is not the same thing as a completed preservation.

Important: A device can have a saved Device Report even when no completed, verified preservation exists. Preservation completion must be established by the preservation result itself.

4. Analyze before applying

When you choose a protection workflow, RLB can analyze the verified retained content and determine what work is required. Analysis is intended to be a preview step: it determines what would need to happen without accessing the original device to perform reconciliation changes.

Review the preview counts and classifications before proceeding to an operation that actually establishes content.

5. Continue into preservation

Preservation is where RLB establishes verified protection for content discovered on the device. Some content can be represented by verified reusable Library content; other content must be retained specifically with that preservation.
