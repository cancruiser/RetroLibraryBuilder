Core protection model

Preserve the device, not just the files.

RLB preservation is designed to establish verified protection for the content and evidence that make a retro device understandable and recoverable. It is deliberately more rigorous than copying an SD card into a folder.

PRESERVATION MODEL
SOURCE DEVICE Original media Boot files, firmware evidence, games, BIOS, media, saves, configuration and device-specific content.
→
RLB VERIFIES What protects each item? Identity and representation are proven rather than inferred from a matching filename or size.

LIBRARY Reusable representation Verified content that can safely represent the source for this and other devices.
PRESERVATION Retained content Verified content kept with this preservation when Library representation cannot be established.

Core principle: An original device is not considered protected merely because files were copied somewhere. RLB must be able to account for the content it intended to protect and the evidence used to establish that protection.

1. What preservation means in RLB

A retro handheld can depend on much more than its ROM folders. The original media may contain boot assets, firmware configuration, DTBs, BIOS files, frontend metadata, artwork, saves and device-specific files that are difficult or impossible to reconstruct later.

RLB preservation therefore asks two questions for the content it discovers:

01Is this already safely represented?If RLB can prove an appropriate verified Library representation, another device-specific copy may not be necessary.
02If not, what must be retained?Content that cannot be safely represented elsewhere stays associated with the preservation.

2. The protection workflow

The exact buttons available depend on the device state, but the normal protection path follows this logic:

1
Inspect and AnalyzeRLB examines the selected source and gathers device, partition and content evidence. Analysis is the evidence-gathering stage.
2
Review what protection requiresRLB determines what is already represented, what needs protection, and what cannot safely be assumed.
3
Run Essential ProtectionWhen prerequisites are satisfied, RLB performs the protection work required for the selected device.
4
Verify the resultCompletion is based on preservation evidence and verification state, not simply the fact that a copy operation ended.
5
Keep the preservation as device historyThe preservation and Device Report carry forward the identity and evidence needed for later recovery and deployment decisions.

3. Analyze and Preview come before Apply

RLB separates analysis from work that establishes new destination content. Where a reconciliation operation is involved, the expected pattern is Analyze → Preview → Confirm → Apply.

The preview is important because it lets the user see what RLB believes will happen before any reconciliation work is approved.

4. Library content and Retained content are different

▦Library content

Verified reusable content maintained independently of a particular preserved device.

  • Can represent content from more than one device.
  • Avoids unnecessary repeated device-specific copies.
  • Must establish the correct content identity, not just a similar path or size.
◆Retained content

Verified content kept with a specific preservation because an appropriate reusable Library representation was not established.

  • Preserves unique or unresolved device content.
  • Remains associated with the preservation.
  • Can later be reconciled if a valid Library representation becomes possible.

5. A canonical representation may not look identical

RLB distinguishes content identity from container identity. A canonical Library ZIP, for example, may legitimately represent a source item even if the ZIP container has different metadata or a different total file length.

What matters is whether RLB can verify the payload relationship required by the representation rule. A container-size comparison alone is not enough.

Example: Repacking the same verified game payload into a canonical ZIP can change ZIP metadata and compressed length without changing the content RLB is trying to represent.

6. Why cryptographic verification matters

Two files can share the same filename and byte length while containing different data. For content identity that matters to preservation, RLB uses cryptographic evidence rather than relying on weak signals alone.

7. Essential Protection is about the original device

Essential Protection is the protection operation for the selected source device. Its purpose is to establish the protection RLB requires before the original media is treated as safely preserved.

The Protection Summary in Device Inspector separates major protection areas such as system/recovery material and user content so an incomplete area remains visible.

Important: Do not remove, erase, repurpose or discard original media merely because a Device Report exists or a previous operation was started. Confirm the actual preservation and verification state first.

8. Retained-content reconciliation does not require the original device

After a preservation exists, RLB can later examine verified retained content and determine whether some of it can now be represented in the reusable Library.

This is a separate reconciliation workflow. It works from the preserved retained content and persisted preservation evidence; the original device does not need to be accessed for that reconciliation.

9. Long-running work can be interrupted

Preservation can involve many thousands of files and may take hours. RLB runs long work through background operations and maintains durable preservation state so that completed work can be distinguished from an interrupted run.

An interrupted operation is not automatically equivalent to a failed preservation, and a stale UI label is not automatically proof of completion. The authoritative question is what the persisted preservation evidence says was actually established and verified.

10. When is it safe to call a device preserved?

For practical use, look for a completed preservation state in which the required protection areas are established and verification has completed successfully.

✓Required content accounted for
✓System / recovery protection established where required
✓User content protection established where required
✓Verification completed
✓No unresolved result is being mistaken for success

Preservation terminology

Analyze
Examines evidence and predicts required work. Analysis itself is not the same as establishing new destination content.
Preview
The user-reviewable plan produced from analysis before a confirmed reconciliation Apply.
Apply
Performs approved reconciliation work and validates the destination conditions encountered while doing so.
Represented
RLB has established an acceptable verified representation of the source content in the appropriate destination context.
Retained
The content remains with the specific preservation rather than being represented solely by reusable Library content.
Verified
The required identity or protection evidence has been checked rather than assumed from filename, path or size alone.
Conflict / Review
A condition that should not be silently treated as established. RLB preserves the distinction so it can be reviewed or resolved by a defined rule.
